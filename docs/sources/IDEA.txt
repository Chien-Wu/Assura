# Build Brief: NDIS Compliance Verification Platform
**Track 1 (Improve an Existing Business Capability) + Built With ElevenLabs**

---

## The idea, in one paragraph

Support workers in Australia's $46B NDIS sector write a shift note after every visit, and since November 2025 providers can face penalties up to $16.5M for compliance failures traced back to those notes. Every existing tool (Clio Care, Noteable, Sparks Scribe, ShiftCare) helps workers *write* a note. None of them tell you whether the note actually satisfies the specific regulatory requirements auditors check for. We built the layer that's missing: a worker dictates their shift, ElevenLabs turns it into a structured note and scores it against the real NDIS compliance checklist in real time, and that feeds a dashboard for the provider's compliance manager — the person who actually gets fined — showing what's audit-ready and what isn't, before the monthly regulatory filing is due.

---

## The problem, with evidence

- NDIS scheme: **$46.3B** in payments (2024-25), 739,414 participants, ~254,000 active unregistered (individual/sole-trader) providers alongside ~16,000 registered organisations.
- The **NDIS Amendment (Integrity and Safeguarding) Act**, in force since late 2025, raised Code of Conduct penalties for breaches causing death/serious injury from $400,000 to **$16.5M**, and general participant-harm civil penalties from $412,500 to **$15M+**.
- Workforce reality driving the documentation problem: **26% casual turnover**, **43% report burnout at least half the time**, permanent full-time roles fell from 30% to 21% of the workforce in one year. This is the workforce being handed the compliance burden — high-turnover, time-pressed, and least likely to have been trained deeply on a new tool.
- Auditors' own named failure modes (from provider compliance guidance): missing goal linkage (**most common gap**), vague/low-content notes, copy-pasted notes across shifts, incidents mentioned but not filed, restrictive practices used but undocumented, notes filed late (past the 24-hour window).

## Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Role |
|---|---|
| **Support worker** | Dictates the shift report — the input side |
| **Registered provider (organisation)** | The customer/buyer; legally liable for documentation quality and the one who pays penalties |

| **NDIS participant** | Subject of the note; privacy stakeholder, not a direct user |
| **Behaviour support practitioner** | Relevant when restrictive practices are involved; owns the Behaviour Support Plan the note must align to |
| **NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission** | The regulator; not a user, but the eventual reader if there's an audit or incident investigation |

## What we're building (Hybrid, dashboard as centerpiece)

Two layers, same underlying pipeline:

1. **Capture layer (the demo's live moment):** worker dictates a shift report → ElevenLabs Scribe v2 Realtime transcribes it → Agents platform extracts structured fields and scores them against the compliance checklist in real time, using Data Collection + Evaluation Criteria (configured, not hand-coded, for speed) alongside our own structured-extraction pipeline run against a small hand-labelled test set (for a genuine, stated accuracy number — this is what earns technical-difficulty points beyond platform configuration).
2. **Oversight layer (the pitch's centerpiece):** a dashboard for the compliance manager showing roster-wide note completeness, which workers/participants have gaps, and — the sharpest feature — restrictive-practice usage patterns flagged *before* the monthly Section 15(2) filing is due, matching the exact pattern the NDIS Commission itself says it scrutinises (repeated/unauthorised use concentrated in a small number of providers or participants).

**Scoping discipline:** one solid worker-dictation flow is enough to prove the pipeline works live on stage — don't build multiple polished worker personas. Spend the majority of remaining hours on the dashboard, since that's where differentiation, business value, and three of the four judges' interests concentrate.

**Why voice has to stay live in the demo:** the ElevenLabs special track requires voice to be core functionality. A dashboard alone, without a live capture moment, risks reading as a bolt-on and failing that track's requirement.

## What "compliance verification" actually checks

The distinct, defensible claim: not "we generate compliant-sounding notes" (everyone does that) but "we check a note against these specific things and tell you what's missing, with a reason."

| Requirement | Common failure mode | What verification catches |
|---|---|---|
| Goal linkage | Most commonly missing element | Flags "no goal reference found" |
| Objective, specific content | Vague notes ("all good," "quiet shift") | Detects low-information notes, prompts for more detail |
| Non-identical language | Copy-pasted notes across shifts (named "major" non-conformance) | Checks against recent note history for near-duplicates |
| Incident disclosure | Incident happens but isn't flagged, or flagged without a matching incident report | Forces explicit incident yes/no on every note; cross-checks for a matching record |
| Restrictive practice documentation | Used but not logged in the register — the single most serious flag | Detects mentions in the transcript, cross-references the register; feeds the provider-side pattern alert |
| Contemporaneous recording | Notes written days late | Solved structurally — dictating right after the shift beats typing later |
| Identifier completeness | Missing date/time/participant/duration | Basic field-completeness check |
| Person-centred language | Clinical/stigmatising phrasing | Lower priority; cheap add if time permits |

## The regulatory clocks this has to respect

| Obligation | Who | Frequency | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Progress/shift note | Worker | Every shift | Within 24 hours |
| Restrictive practice report (Section 15(2)) | Provider | Monthly, per participant (even if "none used") | ~5 business days after month end (confirm exact figure against current NDIS Rules) |
| Reportable incident | Provider | Event-driven | Notify within 24 hours; full report within 5 business days |
| Unauthorised restrictive practice use | Provider | Event-driven | Same two-stage pattern; continues until authorised or added to an approved BSP |

**Compliance filing** (distinct from a note): the actual report a provider submits *to the NDIS Commission* — Section 15(2), or an incident notification — built from the underlying notes. The product doesn't auto-submit these (see below) but auto-assembles a ready-to-review draft from the month's verified notes.

---

## ElevenLabs' role — three jobs, why it's core not decorative

1. **Capture (Scribe v2 Realtime):** hands-free dictation right after a shift, replacing the "I'll type it later" habit that causes late/vague/copy-pasted notes.
2. **Structuring + scoring (Agents — Data Collection + Evaluation Criteria):** extracts structured fields and returns a pass/fail-with-reason per compliance element, largely via platform configuration.
3. **Optional close-the-loop (Flash v2.5 TTS):** the agent can speak back a gap ("no goal link found — want to add one?"), making it a real conversation, not a one-shot recording.

**The test:** replace ElevenLabs with a text box and the product falls apart — a tired worker at the end of a shift won't type a structured note, which is the exact behaviour causing today's gaps. Voice isn't a nicer input method here; it's the only one that gets used.

## Differentiation — named, specific, ready for the slide

| Competitor | What they do | What they don't do |
|---|---|---|
| Clio Care | Generates notes from voice/plain language; goal linking; incident classification (RI1–RI6); Section 15(2) template; privacy filtering | No published scored, criterion-by-criterion verification with a stated accuracy number; targets individual workers, not provider oversight |
| Noteable / Sparks Scribe | Voice/text recap → structured draft; session-to-session memory | Same gap — generation, not verification |
| VisiCase | Voice-to-text is the core feature | Transcribes only; no structured note or compliance check |
| ShiftCare / Vertex360 | Full CMS; AI rephrases bullets into paragraphs | No compliance verification at all |
| **ChatGPT/Claude directly** | Can write a compliant-sounding note if prompted well | No consistent structured scoring, no hands-free workflow embedded in a shift, no roster-wide aggregation, no regulatory-schema-aware filing draft, no appropriate data handling for participant transcripts |

**One-line answer if a judge asks "why not just ChatGPT":** the model call is commoditized — true of nearly every AI project this year. What isn't commoditized is the compliance schema encoded from the actual Practice Standards, a verification harness with a measured accuracy number, a workflow that gets a burnt-out worker to actually use it, and the roster-level aggregation a provider would pay for.

## What we're explicitly NOT building, and why

**No auto-submit to the NDIS Commission.** The Commission's reporting portal is gated by PRODA login with an "Authorised Reporting Officer" role — no public API, unlike the separate claiming API some tools use. Scripting logins into a government portal isn't buildable in 48 hours and isn't something a demo should attempt. More importantly: submitting false/misleading information to the Commission carries penalties up to $3.3M, and that liability sits with the provider — every comparable tool keeps a human in the loop before filing, and so should we. **We auto-assemble a ready-to-review draft; a human still clicks submit.**

## Who's liable (why the provider is the buyer)

| Who | Liable for | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Provider (org) | Code of Conduct breach causing death/serious injury | Up to $16.5M |
| Provider (org) | Participant harm generally | Up to $15M+ |
| Provider (org) | False/misleading info to the Commission | Up to $3.3M |
| Individual | Breaching a banning order | Up to 5 years imprisonment |
| Individual | Unregistered provision requiring registration | Up to 5 years imprisonment |

The multi-million-dollar exposure sits with the organisation, not the worker who wrote one bad note — which is why the compliance manager, not the individual worker, is the right buyer for the dashboard.

## Feasibility for the 48-hour build

- No PHI or real participant data needed — synthetic transcripts and synthetic participant/worker profiles are standard practice and legally clean.
- NDIS Practice Standards, Rules, and Commission guidance are public — usable as a static reference corpus for verification logic.
- No enterprise access, paid data, or partner contract required.

## Key caveats to keep honest in the pitch

- The NDIS Commission's public enforcement register isn't broken down by root cause — don't claim a specific "% of banning orders caused by bad documentation." The defensible claim is the causal chain: undocumented incidents/restrictive practices become reportable incidents, which do show up in the enforcement categories the Commission reports on.
- Some competitor feature claims are vendor-sourced (including from a competitor's own comparison blog) — treat as vendor-reported, not independently verified, if asked directly.
- The exact Section 15(2) filing deadline (days after month-end) varies slightly across sources — confirm against the current NDIS Rules text if precision matters under Q&A.
